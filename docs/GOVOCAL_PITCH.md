# RIOT → the opportunity GoVocal is sleeping on

*A short pitch. Click the demo first, then read.*

🔗 **Try it:** open the live demo, switch the city to **Brussels**, vote on a dozen
real Parliament decisions, and watch where you land against the political groups —
with other people showing up live on the same map.

---

## The uncomfortable bit
That demo took real Brussels-Capital Parliament roll-call votes, digested each one
into something a busy human can actually understand, and let people react and see
each other. You recognise these decisions. That's the point — and it's also the
indictment.

Because here's what we do for a living: we run beautiful participation processes on
the **safe agenda** — the watered-down, low-stakes questions a council's
(politically powerless) participation department is *allowed* to ask. We are very
good at a game that's been rigged to not matter much.

**RIOT runs participation on the real agenda** — the actual plenary decisions, the
contested ones, straight from the public record. Same craft, pointed at where the
power actually is.

## Why it can work where our usual processes stall
The reason participation departments are powerless is that their whole model is
*asking permission*. RIOT doesn't ask — **it measures**:

> "Here's how citizens' proxies would have voted, vs. how the Parliament actually
> voted, vs. each party."

A *finding*, not a request. It runs on public data, needs no council's sign-off, and
once it exists it creates the pressure a polite consultation never could. That's a
product we could own.

## The real vision is two engines (the demo only shows half of one)
1. **Deliberation — what we're already the best at.** A live plenary, each topic
   spinning up a real deliberation space: amendments, trade-offs, *shared facts*, not
   a dumb yes/no. A regional budget is unreadable today; we could make it legible and
   actually discussable. This is GoVocal's core craft, finally aimed at decisions
   that matter, in real time.
2. **Representation — the part that makes #1 scale.** Deliberation costs time
   citizens don't have. So each person gets an **AI proxy** built from their own
   positions that shows up for them — and crucially, **explains itself so they can
   check it.** Over ~100 real Reus votes it already tracks one citizen's will and
   holds up against the parties.

**The unlock is how they fit together:** *deep where you care, represented
everywhere else.* You tag in to join the deliberations you care about (which gathers
the people who actually care into each one); your proxy covers the rest, so you're
never just absent — you're heard, by something you can audit, instead of only by a
politician you didn't pick for this. Politicians get a deliberated, explained,
pre-vote signal instead of a tally.

## Why *us*, and why now
We have the rarest half already: people who know how to gather citizens, ask the
right question, and hold a deliberative space. The engine (digest the real agenda +
the explainable proxy) is the half RIOT shows is buildable — the Brussels demo took
days. Nobody is better positioned to put these two halves together than a
deliberation company that also builds the tech. If we don't, someone with worse
instincts about legitimacy will.

## What's real vs. what's next
- **Real today:** multi-city demo (Reus + Brussels), real digested votes,
  live multiplayer map. Playable, shareable, recognisable.
- **Two prototypes away from the full story:**
  - a **replay** of one real plenary on a timer, each topic opening its deliberation
    space — shows the live-deliberation future without the hard infrastructure;
  - **in-app proxy onboarding** — a short profile → your own explainable ghost.

## The ask
Half a day in a room. Not to admire the gimmick, but to decide whether
*influencing how governance actually happens* is a bigger prize than the next
increment on the service we already sell. The demo exists so the conversation can
start from something real instead of a slide.
