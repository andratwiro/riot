MP pay · Proposal for a decision

Cut Brussels MPs' parliamentary indemnity by 5% on a permanent basis, applying the reduction not only to the base pay but also to the extra indemnities paid for special functions.

On the table is a standalone proposal to reduce the indemnity Brussels regional MPs draw by 5% as a lasting measure, and to push that cut further than the parallel cross-party text by also shaving 5% off the top-up indemnities paid to members who hold special functions (such as Bureau seats).

  • What it changes: a 5% reduction on the base parliamentary indemnity, framed as permanent rather than time-limited, plus the same 5% applied to special-function indemnities.
  • The money cited: speakers describe a Brussels MP's net monthly pay as more than 7,000 euros, and special-function holders receiving roughly 2,500 euros extra.
  • The wider frame: it lands amid a regional budget described as a 1.6 to 1.9 billion euro deficit, with cuts cited to associations (15%) and family allowances.
  • The rub: whether elected members should cut their own pay deeply and permanently as a credibility signal, or whether a 5% trim risks underpaying the job and should align with other parliaments rather than go furthest.

Public finance · Budget

Adopt the Region's 2026 spending budget, authorising the year's expenditure and the deficit that comes with it.

This is the spending side of the Region's 2026 budget — the credits each policy area is authorised to spend. Paired with the revenue text, it is the Region's first full initial budget after a long run on provisional twelfths.

  • It sets the deficit. The 2026 budget is planned to close in the red, with the SEC/ESR balance fixed at under -957 million euros.
  • From stopgap to plan. For more than a year the Region spent through monthly provisional twelfths, which guaranteed continuity of public services but rolled deficits forward without a clear direction.
  • Savings land on real programmes. Debate flagged cuts touching employment measures — reduced subsidised-contract premiums and the end of the Activa scheme among them.
  • The rub: the only ways to shrink a 1.5-billion-euro deficit are to cut spending or raise revenue — and where the axe falls (employment, social programmes) is exactly what the budget is fought over.
